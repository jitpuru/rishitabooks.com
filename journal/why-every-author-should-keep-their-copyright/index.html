Ownership

Why Every Author Should Keep Their Copyright

Rishita Books · 4 min read

The single most important decision an author makes has nothing to do with word count, cover design or launch dates. It is a quieter question, and it is easy to overlook until it is too late: when your book is published, who actually owns it — you, or someone else?

Many first-time authors are surprised to learn that with some publishers and “done-for-you” services, signing a contract can mean signing away the rights to their own words. The book carries your name, your stories, your hard-won expertise — but the ownership sits with a third party.

What copyright actually protects

Copyright is your legal ownership of the work you created. It governs who can print, sell, translate, adapt or license the book — and who earns from it. If you hold your copyright, those decisions, and the income they generate, remain yours for the life of the work.

Why it matters more than you think

Your book is rarely just a book. It is a calling card, a lead generator and the foundation for talks, courses, workshops and future editions. If you do not own it, you may find yourself asking permission — or losing revenue — every time you want to build on your own ideas.

At Rishita Books, authors keep full copyright to their work. Always. We bring the craft, standards and worldwide distribution of a publishing house, while the work itself stays entirely yours.

So before you sign anything, ask one simple question: “When this is published, do I still own it?” If the answer is not a clear and unambiguous yes, keep asking until it is.
